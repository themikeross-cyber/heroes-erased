Terrible Terry

Major General Terry de la Mesa Allen Sr. The Big Red One, then the Timberwolves.

North Africa, Sicily & Germany · 1888–1969

Biography

Terry de la Mesa Allen Sr. was born in 1888 at Fort Douglas, Utah, into a family already marked by military service. He entered West Point in 1907 and left without a diploma, finished a degree at the Catholic University of America, and earned a cavalry commission by examination in 1912. In France in 1918 he led a battalion of the 90th Division through St. Mihiel and the Meuse-Argonne. He came home with a Silver Star, two wounds, and a settled belief that a short surprise attack at night saves lives.

In 1942 he took command of the 1st Infantry Division. Through North Africa and Sicily the Big Red One fought hard and, off the line, celebrated the same way. Allen and his assistant commander, Theodore Roosevelt Jr., cared more about combat than polish. General Omar Bradley relieved them both in August 1943. Patton, who had wanted Allen for the hardest landing in Sicily, told Eisenhower that no one should whip a dog before putting him into a fight. Allen wrote afterward that the division had come through ten months of combat successfully, and that he was glad to leave it in capable hands.

That October he received the 104th Infantry Division, the Timberwolves, and trained them to attack at night until Bradley — the general who had relieved him — called them the only division he knew that was prepared for night combat. The 104th landed in France in September 1944 and fought for 195 days, from the Netherlands across the Rhine to the Mulde River. Allen kept his headquarters forward and slept on the ground. He retired in 1946 and died in El Paso in 1969.

Medals & Awards

  • Army Distinguished Service Medal Oak leaf cluster
  • Silver Star
  • Legion of Merit
  • Purple Heart Oak leaf cluster
  • French Legion of Honor
  • French Croix de Guerre with palm
  • Honorary Companion of the Order of the Bath
  • Order of Suvorov, Second Class

Notable Achievements

  • Commanded the 1st Infantry Division in North Africa and Sicily, from May 1942 to August 1943.
  • Led a battalion in the Meuse-Argonne in 1918 and came out of that war convinced of the night attack.
  • Trained the 104th Infantry Division to fight at night, then led it for 195 days in Northwest Europe.
  • Appeared on the cover of Time magazine on August 9, 1943.
  • Kept the trust of enlisted soldiers by staying forward and judging men by how they fought.
