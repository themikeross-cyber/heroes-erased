99th Infantry Division

Lyle Bouck

First Lieutenant Lyle J. Bouck Jr. Intelligence and Reconnaissance Platoon, 394th Infantry.

Lanzerath, Belgium · December 16, 1944

Biography

Lyle Bouck Jr. was twenty, and he led a platoon that was not organized to fight a pitched battle. The Intelligence and Reconnaissance Platoon of the 394th Infantry, 99th Division, was a scouting outfit of eighteen men, trained to watch a road and report what moved on it. In early December 1944 they were placed on a ridge above the Belgian village of Lanzerath, on a quiet part of the Ardennes. Before dawn on December 16 the quiet ended. German artillery opened the offensive that became the Battle of the Bulge, and a parachute regiment leading a panzer corps came up the road.

Bouck’s men, with a handful of artillery observers, stayed in their foxholes and stopped the column. They turned back attack after attack. The Distinguished Service Cross citation later credited the platoon with delaying the advance for eighteen hours. When the ammunition was gone, the position was overrun and the survivors were captured. Bouck was wounded. The next day, December 17, he turned twenty-one as a prisoner of war.

The medals did not come with that winter. In October 1981 the Army honored the platoon for Lanzerath. Bouck received the Distinguished Service Cross. Others received the Distinguished Service Cross, the Silver Star, or the Bronze Star. He had been recommended at the time for the Medal of Honor. The wait was thirty-seven years. The action is now taught as a study in what a small unit, well led, can do on ground that matters.

Medals & Awards

  • Distinguished Service Cross Awarded 1981
  • Silver Star
  • Purple Heart Two oak leaf clusters
  • Prisoner of War Medal

Notable Achievements

  • On December 16, 1944, his eighteen-man platoon held a ridge above Lanzerath.
  • Delayed a lead element of the German offensive for about eighteen hours.
  • Turned back repeated assaults before the platoon was overrun and captured.
  • Spent his twenty-first birthday, the next day, as a prisoner of war.
  • Received the Distinguished Service Cross in 1981, after a recommendation for the Medal of Honor.
