Merrill’s Marauders

Fightin’ Preacher

Logan E. Weston. Infantry officer with the 37th Division and the 5307th Composite Unit.

New Georgia & Burma · 1942–1944

Biography

Logan E. Weston left Transylvania Bible School in Pennsylvania to enlist. He fought first as a soldier and then as a young officer with the 37th Infantry Division in the Solomon Islands. On New Georgia his platoon gave him the name he carried for the rest of his life. He kept up his scripture when the line was quiet. When the firing started, he was in front. His men said that in a fight he was as aggressive as any platoon leader they knew, and that afterward he would gather them and say he was sorry for the killing the day had required.

He took the name into Merrill’s Marauders, the 5307th Composite Unit, which walked into Burma in 1944 to operate behind Japanese lines. Weston led reconnaissance soldiers of that force, including the fight around the supply base at Walawbum. The Marauders were volunteers, short of food and rest, and asked to do the work of a much larger unit. He led them as he had led the platoon on New Georgia: a prayer, then the advance.

The nickname followed him into two later wars. In Korea he commanded a rifle company and received the Distinguished Service Cross and the Silver Star. He served again in Vietnam with Special Forces. In 1996 the Army inducted Colonel Weston into the Ranger Hall of Fame. The name his platoon chose in the Pacific was the one that lasted.

Medals & Awards

  • Distinguished Service Cross Korea, 1950
  • Silver Star Korea, 1950

Notable Achievements

  • Left Bible school to enlist and fought with the 37th Infantry Division on New Georgia.
  • Earned the name Fightin’ Preacher from the men of his own platoon.
  • Served with Merrill’s Marauders in Burma, including the action at Walawbum.
  • Led with scripture in the quiet hours and from the front once the attack began.
  • Inducted into the Ranger Hall of Fame in 1996.
