442nd Regimental Combat Team

Go For Broke Boys

Nisei soldiers of the 100th Infantry Battalion and the 442nd. Their motto was a gambler’s phrase: risk everything.

Italy, France & Germany · 1943–1945

Biography

In 1943 the War Department asked for volunteers to form a combat unit of Japanese-American soldiers. More than twelve thousand answered. The men who were selected, Nisei from Hawaii and from the mainland incarceration camps, trained at Camp Shelby, Mississippi. They took their motto from the dice table. Go For Broke. Put everything on the line.

The 100th Infantry Battalion was already in Italy when the 442nd Regimental Combat Team arrived to join it. Together they fought up the Italian peninsula, in southern France, and into Germany. In the Vosges Mountains in late October 1944, the regiment was sent to break through to the 1st Battalion of the 141st Infantry, cut off in the forest and soon called the Lost Battalion. The rescue succeeded. The losses were severe. Soldiers whose own families were held behind barbed wire in the United States kept moving forward.

The decorations earned in less than two years are among the most extraordinary in the Army for a unit of this size. Twenty-one soldiers of the regiment and its attached battalion ultimately received the Medal of Honor. In 2010 Congress awarded the Congressional Gold Medal to the 442nd and the units that served with it. April 5 is observed as Go For Broke Day.

Medals & Awards

  • Presidential Unit Citation Seven awards
  • Congressional Gold Medal Awarded by Congress in 2010
  • 21 Medals of Honor Awarded to individual soldiers
  • More than 4,000 Bronze Star Medals
  • More than 4,000 Purple Hearts

Notable Achievements

  • Formed in 1943 from Japanese-American volunteers, many with families in U.S. incarceration camps.
  • Fought in Italy, southern France, and Germany with the motto “Go For Broke.”
  • In October 1944, rescued the surrounded Lost Battalion in the Vosges Mountains.
  • Recognized as the most decorated American unit of its size for its months in combat.
  • Honored nationally on Go For Broke Day, April 5.
