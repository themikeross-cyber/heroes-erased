Limping Lady

Virginia Hall. American agent in occupied France, known to the Gestapo by her limp.

France · 1906–1982

Biography

Virginia Hall wanted to be a diplomat. A hunting accident in Turkey in 1933 cost her the left leg below the knee, and with it the State Department career she had been building. She named the wooden leg Cuthbert and went to war by another door. In 1941 the British Special Operations Executive sent her into Vichy France. She built networks, moved papers and money, and stayed long enough for the Gestapo to learn her walk. They called her the limping lady and placed her among the agents they most wanted to catch.

In the autumn of 1942 she walked over the Pyrenees into Spain. Before she left, she signaled London that she hoped Cuthbert would not give her trouble. London missed the joke and replied that if Cuthbert became troublesome, she should eliminate him. Spain jailed her for crossing the border. The American embassy secured her release. Britain made her an honorary Member of the Order of the British Empire, and the SOE declined to send her back. She was too well known.

The Office of Strategic Services would send her. In March 1944 she landed in Brittany by boat, disguised as an older countrywoman, and spent the summer arming resistance bands in central and southern France. Her groups cut roads and pressed the German retreat. In September 1945, General William Donovan presented the Distinguished Service Cross in a private ceremony. She was the only civilian woman to receive it during the war. President Truman wanted the moment to be public. Hall refused. She was, she said, still operational. She later served in the CIA and rarely spoke about France. She died in Maryland in 1982.

Medals & Awards

  • Distinguished Service Cross
  • Member of the Order of the British Empire Honorary
  • Croix de Guerre with palm France

Notable Achievements

  • Organized resistance circuits in Vichy France for the Special Operations Executive.
  • Walked across the Pyrenees in 1942 on a wooden leg she called Cuthbert.
  • Returned with the OSS in 1944 and armed Maquis groups before the liberation of southern France.
  • The only civilian woman awarded the Distinguished Service Cross in the Second World War.
  • Declined a public White House ceremony, saying she was still operational.
