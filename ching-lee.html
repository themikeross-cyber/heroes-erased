United States Navy

Ching Lee

Vice Admiral Willis Augustus Lee Jr. Battleship commander and Olympic rifleman.

Natlee, Kentucky · Guadalcanal · 1888–1945

Biography

Willis Augustus Lee Jr. picked up the nickname Ching as a midshipman, from a surname classmates found unusual and from an interest in the Far East he never dropped. It stayed with him to the rank of vice admiral. Before he commanded battleships he was a rifleman of rare skill. In 1907, still a teenager, he won both the national high-power rifle and pistol championships. At the 1920 Olympics in Antwerp he returned with seven medals, five of them gold.

His war turned on a dark night off Guadalcanal. On November 14–15, 1942, Rear Admiral Lee flew his flag in the battleship Washington. A Japanese force built around the battleship Kirishima was coming down to shell the Marines and put reinforcements ashore. Lee fought the action with radar, an instrument he understood as an extension of gunnery. Washington’s salvos left Kirishima a wreck. She sank before morning. It was the only time in the war an American battleship sank an enemy battleship in a single-ship gunfight.

The Navy Cross followed, and in 1944 command of the Pacific Fleet’s battleships. Ten days after Japan surrendered, Lee died of a heart attack in a launch carrying him out to the gunnery ship Wyoming, off Portland, Maine. He is buried at Arlington National Cemetery.

Medals & Awards

  • Navy Cross
  • Navy Distinguished Service Medal Two awards
  • Legion of Merit
  • Five gold, one silver, and one bronze medal 1920 Olympic Games, Antwerp

Notable Achievements

  • Won the U.S. national high-power rifle and pistol championships in the same year, 1907.
  • Tied the record for medals at a single Olympics, a mark that stood for sixty years.
  • On November 14–15, 1942, directed the gunfire that sank the battleship Kirishima.
  • Fought that night action primarily by radar from his flagship, USS Washington.
  • Later commanded the fast battleships of the Pacific Fleet.
