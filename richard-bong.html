United States Army Air Forces

Richard Bong

Major. America’s ace of aces, forty victories in the Pacific.

Poplar, Wisconsin · 1920–1945

Biography

Richard Ira Bong grew up on a farm in Poplar, Wisconsin, one of nine children. He learned to fly through the Civilian Pilot Training Program, earned his wings in January 1942, and went to the Southwest Pacific as a P-38 Lightning pilot. General George Kenney already knew the name. In training over California, Bong had flown so low that Kenney grounded him, then decided that same nerve belonged in combat.

Over New Guinea and later the Philippines, Bong became the highest-scoring American fighter pilot of the war. All forty of his confirmed victories came in the Lightning. Squadron mates remembered a pilot who closed until missing was unlikely, and who sometimes damaged an enemy aircraft and left the finish to a wingman. When Kenney promised him leave after twenty victories, Bong timed the last of them for Wisconsin’s deer season. He named his fighter Marge, for Marjorie Vattendahl, and married her in 1945.

He received the Medal of Honor from General Douglas MacArthur in December 1944 and was sent home, too valuable to risk on another combat tour. The Army put him to work on war bonds and then on jet tests for Lockheed. On August 6, 1945, the fuel pump failed on a P-80 Shooting Star during takeoff at Burbank. He left the aircraft too low for the parachute to open. He was twenty-four. The newspapers carried his death beside the news from Hiroshima.

Medals & Awards

  • Medal of Honor
  • Distinguished Service Cross
  • Silver Star
  • Distinguished Flying Cross
  • Air Medal

Notable Achievements

  • Forty confirmed aerial victories, every one in a Lockheed P-38 Lightning.
  • Passed Eddie Rickenbacker’s American record from the First World War.
  • Flew with the 49th Fighter Group and later on the staff of V Fighter Command.
  • Received the Medal of Honor from General Douglas MacArthur in December 1944.
  • Known for giving wingmen the chance to finish a damaged enemy aircraft.
