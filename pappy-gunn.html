Fifth Air Force

Pappy Gunn

Paul Irvin Gunn. Pilot, mechanic, and the man who armed the noses of Kenney’s bombers.

Southwest Pacific · 1899–1957

Biography

Paul Irvin Gunn had already lived one flying career when the war asked him for another. A naval aviator who had left the service, he was in the Philippines when Japanese forces arrived in 1941. His family was captured and interned. Gunn remained in the theater, accepted a commission in the Army Air Forces, and turned his mechanic’s mind to a blunt problem: American bombers were meeting ships and troops with too little firepower pointed forward.

Working for General George Kenney, he fitted .50-caliber machine guns into the noses of A-20 and B-25 bombers and helped crews learn to attack at mast height. The improvised gunships took a leading part in the Battle of the Bismarck Sea in March 1943, when Allied aircraft destroyed a convoy bound for Lae. North American Aviation later built versions of his idea into the B-25 on the production line. Gunn was decades older than most of the men who flew the aircraft he rebuilt, and they called him Pappy.

He survived the war and returned to the Philippines. He died in a civilian air crash there on October 11, 1957, a week short of his fifty-eighth birthday.

Medals & Awards

  • Silver Star
  • Distinguished Flying Cross
  • Legion of Merit
  • Air Medal
  • Purple Heart

Notable Achievements

  • Converted A-20 and B-25 bombers into low-level strafers with forward-firing .50-caliber guns.
  • Those gunships helped destroy a Japanese reinforcement convoy in the Battle of the Bismarck Sea.
  • Flew early supply missions toward the Philippines, including the Royce mission of April 1942.
  • Became General Kenney’s officer for special projects in the Southwest Pacific.
  • Saw his nose-gun concept adopted on later factory-built B-25s.
