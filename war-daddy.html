3rd Armored Division

War Daddy

Staff Sergeant Lafayette G. Pool. Tank commander, Company I, 32nd Armored Regiment.

Texas · France, Belgium & Germany · 1944

Biography

Lafayette G. Pool came into the Army from Texas and started as a tank mechanic. He boxed, learned the Sherman from the inside, and worked his way into the turret. By the summer of 1944 he was a staff sergeant in Company I, 32nd Armored Regiment, 3rd Armored Division, the Spearhead. His crew called him War Daddy. The tank he rode was named In the Mood. When that Sherman was knocked out, the next one took the same name.

From late June into September 1944, Pool’s crew led attacks across France and Belgium and into Germany. In about eighty-one days of combat he is credited with 12 enemy tanks and 258 other armored vehicles and self-propelled guns, fought from three different Shermans. The Distinguished Service Cross for August 29–31 describes him in the lead tank, accounting for four tanks, three anti-tank guns, and about fifty unarmored vehicles in that stretch, while the task force kept moving.

Near Stolberg, Germany, in September, German fire blew him from the turret of In the Mood III. He lost a leg. He told a Stars and Stripes reporter that he was just getting started. The Army took him back after the war. He retired as a chief warrant officer and died in 1990. He had been recommended for the Medal of Honor. The decoration he received was the Distinguished Service Cross.

Medals & Awards

  • Distinguished Service Cross
  • Silver Star
  • Legion of Merit
  • Purple Heart
  • French Legion of Honor
  • French Croix de Guerre

Notable Achievements

  • Commanded three Shermans, each named In the Mood, as the point tank in repeated attacks.
  • Credited with 12 tanks and 258 armored vehicles and self-propelled guns in about eighty-one days.
  • On August 29–31, 1944, his lead tank cleared guns and vehicles that were blocking the task force.
  • Continued in the Army after losing a leg, and retired as a chief warrant officer.
  • Remembered in the armored force as the American tank ace of the European campaign.
