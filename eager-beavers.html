43rd Bomb Group

Eager Beavers

Captain Jay Zeamer Jr. and the crew of the B-17E Old 666.

Buka & Bougainville · June 16, 1943

Biography

Jay Zeamer Jr. wanted a crew of his own and, for a long time, could not get one. He had trouble checking out as a first pilot, and he carried a reputation as a man other fliers liked and did not quite trust on a landing. In Australia he and a knot of volunteers salvaged a beaten-up B-17E, armed it with extra machine guns, and took the missions other crews hoped to avoid. They called themselves the Eager Beavers. The airplane was Old 666.

On June 16, 1943, Zeamer volunteered to photograph the coasts of Buka and Bougainville ahead of an Allied landing. The bomber made the mapping run alone. Japanese fighters rose to meet it, and Zeamer held the course until the cameras finished. The fight ran for most of an hour. Bombardier Joseph Sarnoski, already wounded, crawled back to his guns and kept firing until he was killed. Zeamer was hit in both arms and both legs, with one leg broken, and stayed at the controls. The crew shot down several of the fighters. Old 666, riddled and without hydraulics, reached Dobodura, hundreds of miles away, and landed.

Zeamer lived. Sarnoski received the Medal of Honor after his death, and Zeamer received the same medal. Every other member of the crew received the Distinguished Service Cross. It remains the most highly decorated aircrew in American history.

Medals & Awards

  • Medal of Honor Captain Jay Zeamer Jr.
  • Medal of Honor 2nd Lt. Joseph R. Sarnoski, posthumous
  • Distinguished Service Cross The other seven members of the crew
  • Purple Heart For wounds on the June 16 mission

Notable Achievements

  • Rebuilt a wrecked B-17E, added extra guns, and volunteered for the dangerous photo missions.
  • On June 16, 1943, mapped Buka and Bougainville while under continuous fighter attack.
  • Brought the film home and landed the damaged bomber at Dobodura.
  • Sarnoski returned to his guns after being mortally wounded.
  • Became the most highly decorated aircrew in American history for a single mission.
